Fighting Back Against Parkinson’s Disease in Thousand Oaks

Our non-contact boxing-based classes are specifically adapted for people living with Parkinson’s disease and related movement disorders.

Every class combines purposeful exercise, cognitive challenges, balance training, voice work, agility drills, and community support.

Whether you were recently diagnosed or have been living with Parkinson’s for many years, our goal is simple:

Help you move better. Help you stay independent. Help you fight back.

Dean has been doing martial arts since age 5, was a competitive fighter and has had the privilege of working with over 500 people with Parkinson’s in the last decade.

For 10 years, Dean has helped individuals improve balance, mobility, strength, and confidence through Rock Steady Boxing.

His mission is simple:

Help people move better, feel stronger, and maintain independence through engaging exercise that is both effective and enjoyable.

Why Parkinson’s Specific Exercise Matters

Research continues to demonstrate that regular exercise can help improve many symptoms associated with Parkinson’s disease.

Our classes focus on:

  • Balance
  • Coordination
  • Reaction time
  • Gait and walking mechanics
  • Strength
  • Flexibility
  • Cognitive engagement
  • Voice projection
  • Confidence

Every workout is adapted to the individual participant.

No boxing experience is required.

What Happens During a Class

Warm Up

Participants begin with movements designed to improve posture, flexibility, mobility, and circulation.

Strength and Conditioning

Functional exercises improve endurance and support activities of daily living.

Boxing

Non-contact boxing drills challenge coordination, reaction time, rhythm, focus, and movement patterns.

Balance and Agility Training

Exercises are incorporated to help reduce fall risk and improve confidence with everyday movement.

Cognitive Challenges

Brain-body exercises help participants practice multitasking, concentration, memory, and processing speed.

Cool Down

Stretching and mobility work help participants finish feeling energized and accomplished.

Frequently Asked Questions
Do I need boxing experience?

No. Most participants have never boxed before joining.

Is this a contact sport?

No. Classes are completely non-contact.

Can beginners participate?

Absolutely. Exercises can be modified for varying abilities.

What should I wear?

Comfortable workout clothing and athletic shoes.

How often should I attend?

Many participants attend multiple times each week for maximum benefit.

What if I struggle with balance or use a walker?

Classes can be done seated or standing and we provide safe modifications for all levels of ability.
